Pros and Cons of Energy & Commodities

Why traders add commodities to a portfolio, and the risks that come with them.

+

Pros

  • DiversificationCommodity prices are driven by supply and demand, not tech earnings, so they often move differently from stock indices.
  • Inflation hedgeEnergy and metals prices often rise with inflation, which can offset losses elsewhere in a portfolio.
  • Scheduled catalystsWeekly EIA inventory reports and OPEC+ meetings create regular, predictable moments of volatility.
  • Dividend incomeLarge energy producers such as Exxon Mobil and Chevron have long histories of paying dividends.
  • Two-way opportunityPrices react quickly to news in both directions, giving traders chances in rising and falling markets.
−

Cons

  • Geopolitical shocksWars, sanctions and supply disruptions can move oil and gas prices sharply and without warning.
  • Extreme volatilityIn April 2020, the front-month WTI futures contract settled at about -$37 a barrel as storage ran out.
  • Weather and seasonalityNatural gas prices swing with winter heating and summer cooling demand, and forecasts change quickly.
  • Roll costsFutures-based funds such as USO and UNG can lag the spot price over time because of the cost of rolling contracts.
  • Policy and transition riskRegulation, production quotas and the shift to renewable energy can change the long-term outlook for the sector.
Strategy

Tactics

Common approaches traders use with energy and commodity markets.

01

Trading the EIA report

Weekly crude inventories (Wednesday) and gas storage (Thursday) often trigger sharp moves. Plan entries and exits around the release.

02

WTI vs. Brent spread

Trade the price gap between the U.S. and global crude benchmarks as supply and shipping conditions change.

03

Energy stocks vs. crude

Compare producers such as XLE, XOM and CVX against the oil price to find shares lagging or leading the commodity.
